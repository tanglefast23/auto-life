## The three simplifications (found by rendering, not by reading)

`deriveSlim` took three iterations. Every failure was invisible in the code and obvious in the silhouette band — which is the entire argument for A0 existing.

**1. Narrowing every torso-band shape destroyed the limbs.** An `outlined()` arm is a 3px shadow box around a 1px fill. Narrowing it by one column per side produced two disconnected 1px columns, and the sheet showed dotted, broken arms. → `SLIM_MIN_WIDTH = 6`: shapes thinner than that are structural and exempt from narrowing.

**2. With the floor alone, slim became indistinguishable from average** — 590 vs 592 opaque px. On a 32px chibi the *outer* silhouette is set by the arms, and the arms are exactly what the floor exempts. → thin shapes are **translated inward** instead of narrowed. Still an offset, still no redraw, and the difference now lands where it reads.

**3. Translating inward merged the stance.** A front-facing walk puts each leg one pixel off the centre line, so pulling both inward collided them into a single 7px block and the silhouette lost the legs entirely. → translation applies only **above `SLIM_LEG_TOP = 35`**. A slim build has narrower shoulders and tucked arms; its stance is unchanged.

All three are regression-tested (`slim never breaks a limb into disconnected columns`, `slim never merges the stance`, `slim leaves the head untouched`).

**Also changed during the spike:** the side facings originally reused the front geometry, so all four directions had byte-identical silhouettes. Left/right now use profile geometry — 11px head, 8px torso, one visible arm, legs stacked front-to-back — giving 10px vs 16px measured torso width.

## Accepted limitation (recorded, not fixed)

**Up and down share a byte-identical silhouette.** A symmetric chibi cannot express toward/away in outline; this is normal for the genre and not worth breaking the proportions over. It is asserted as a test so it can never drift silently into a surprise.

**Consequence for P3, which is why it is recorded here:** direction is carried entirely by the **face layer**. The face and hair layers are therefore load-bearing for legibility and **cannot be dropped from the P3 Atlas as a "placeholder simplification."** A P3 renderer that draws bodies only will show a sim whose facing is unreadable.

## Claims now proven

1. **One authored hair overlay rides every frame via the head anchor.** The seated frame drops the head 6px; the same 12 hair shapes land exactly 6px lower with zero per-frame authoring (asserted numerically). This is the v6 cost claim — 10 hair styles cost 10 sprites, not 10 × 48 frames.
2. **Slim is offset-derived, never redrawn.** One mechanical function, three documented clauses, measurable difference, limbs and stance intact.
3. **Silhouettes read in flat Ink** for pose and for front-vs-profile; not for toward-vs-away (above).
4. **Outfits key to body frames** and one authored set covers a walk loop via `frameBob`.
